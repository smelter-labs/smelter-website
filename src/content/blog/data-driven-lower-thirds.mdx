Northwind covers local sports on a small crew. Their graphics used to lag the
action by a beat — an operator retyping the score into a graphics box after
every goal, always a few seconds behind the whistle.

## The graphics subscribe to the data

Now the scoreboard, game clock, and lower-thirds are React components bound to
the venue's timing feed. When the clock ticks or the score changes, the data
updates and the overlay re-renders on the next frame. Nobody retypes anything;
the graphics are a view of the live data.

The program feed comes in over WHIP, the graphics composite on top in the scene
graph, and the finished broadcast goes back out over WHEP — one pipeline, no
dedicated graphics machine.

## The payoff for a small crew

- Scores and clocks are always in sync with the game, not a few seconds behind.
- One operator runs the whole broadcast instead of driving a graphics box.
- New graphics — a stat bug, a sponsor card — ship as components between games.

The crew stopped chasing the scoreboard and started spending that attention on
the actual coverage.
