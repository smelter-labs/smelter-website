Point a few phone cameras around a room and you have a working AI security
setup — no extra hardware, no off-the-shelf NVR software.

## The pipeline

Each camera sends its stream over WHIP into a Smelter compositing engine on the
server. Smelter merges them into one output you watch live over WHEP, while a
bit of server-side Python scores motion in real time.

Cross the threshold and three things happen: the focus jumps to wherever the
movement is, the recorder kicks in, and the clip lands as an MP4. Gemini 2.5
Flash then watches the clip back, writes up what happened, and assigns a
severity rating. If it's serious enough, a Web Push notification fires — even
with the tab closed. Recordings, analyses, settings, and camera names all
persist in SQLite.

## Swap the inputs, change one prompt

The fun part is how little it takes to repoint the whole thing. Dropping a few
music videos into the local inputs folder and tweaking one line of the Gemini
prompt — to flag the most chaotic moments — was enough to get the same
pipeline scoring dance routines and pyrotechnics instead of someone walking
through the kitchen. Same engine, same pipeline, same handful of files.

The app grew out of Patryk's idea at a Software Mansion × Gemini hackathon
earlier this year. As of the Smelter release that landed shortly before the
episode, this kind of pipeline is supported natively.

See it running in the [live demo](https://workshop.smelter.dev).
