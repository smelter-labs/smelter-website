Nadia hosts a weekly live show where guests drop in and out mid-episode. The
old setup meant a producer manually swapping scenes in a switcher every time
someone joined — and a visible cut each time they got it wrong.

## One layout function, any number of guests

Guests connect over WHIP. Each new input is just another entry in an array,
and the layout is a plain function of how many are live: solo gets a full
frame, two split the screen, three-to-six tile into a grid. When a guest
leaves, the array shrinks and the scene re-tiles on the next frame.

Because the layout is code, the host never touches a switcher. Joining and
leaving is driven by connection state, not by a person clicking the right
source at the right second.

## What it buys the show

- No producer babysitting a switcher during the live show.
- Every transition is animated, so guests appear and disappear cleanly.
- The same layout logic runs in rehearsal and on air — no scene to rebuild.

The show went from "hope the producer is paying attention" to a pipeline that
just reacts to who's on the call.
