Episode two builds a complete Pong from a single prompt — and then does the
thing that makes it a Smelter demo rather than a game jam entry: the live input
video renders *inside the ball*.

## A real game, not a loop

Both paddles switch between keyboard, mouse, and AI control, the difficulty is
adjustable, and the match length is configurable. Deflection angles depend on
where the ball hits the paddle — the same trick the 1972 cabinet used.

The whole thing is painted per-pixel on the GPU in one shader and driven from
React over a low-latency WebSocket. What you play is exactly what viewers see;
there is no separate render path for the stream.

## Something to put on the BRB screen

It takes the usual inputs — phone cameras, live streams, pre-recorded files —
so it drops into a tournament intermission, a charity stream, or a BRB screen
and gives viewers a real match instead of a placeholder card.
