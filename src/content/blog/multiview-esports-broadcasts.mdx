Riftline used to run its tournament broadcasts on a rack of SDI switchers,
scalers, and a dedicated multiviewer. Every new overlay meant a firmware
update and a trip to the venue. When they moved to Smelter, the whole chain
collapsed into a single programmable pipeline that runs in the browser.

## The setup

Twelve player cams, two observer feeds, and a caster PiP are pulled in as
WHIP inputs, composited into a scene graph, and pushed back out over WHEP —
all at 1080p60 with sub-second glass-to-glass latency.

The overlays — scoreboards, lower-thirds, the bracket — are ordinary React
components. When a caster switches the active player, it's a state update, not
a hardware cut.

## What changed

- No more SDI rack to truck to each venue.
- Overlays ship as code and are versioned in git.
- The same pipeline runs locally for rehearsal and in the cloud for the live show.

Smelter didn't just replace the hardware — it made the whole broadcast
something the engineering team could iterate on between matches.
