Point a camera at the sky. Run YOLO on the decoded frames through Smelter's
side channel. Spawn a NES-style duck sprite on every real bird it finds. Then
shoot them down — with a pistol built from a saw handle, a glow-stick tube, a
bike phone mount, and a Bluetooth trigger button.

## Your phone is the gun

Scan a QR code and you're in: no app, no pairing. Aiming is gyro-mouse, which
is where the first real problem showed up — orientation angles gimbal-lock the
moment you hold a phone like a pistol.

Duck flight is deterministic: the server and the renderer compute the same
path, so every shot lands exactly where you see the sprite rather than where
the server thought it was a frame ago.

## The full NES death beat

White flash, freeze-frame, falling duck — and the dog, if you bag two in two
seconds.

Multiplayer works out of the box: per-player crosshairs, a live scoreboard, and
magazine size and reload time set by the operator. Everything composites and
broadcasts live over WebRTC with Smelter.

Try it in the [live demo](https://workshop.smelter.dev).
