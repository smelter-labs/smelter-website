The first episode of the Smelter Workshop starts where most demos end: with a
prompt. Piotr describes a real-time video carousel to the Smelter Editor, and
what comes back is a working, controllable scene — not a mockup.

## What the prompt produced

The carousel navigates with the arrow keys, and the number of visible inputs,
the animation speed, and the on-screen position are all configurable. There is
even a clap-to-switch toggle, so the scene advances on a sound cue instead of a
keypress.

It works with the same input types everything else in Smelter does: phone
cameras, live streams, and pre-recorded files. Nothing about the carousel is
tied to where the video came from.

## Composed on the GPU, controlled from React

Everything composites on the GPU while the controls stay in ordinary React
state. That split is the point of the whole series — the layout is a component
tree you can read and change, and the pixels are somebody else's problem.

Try it in the [live demo](https://workshop.smelter.dev), or read the source of
the editor itself in
[smelter-labs/smelter-editor](https://github.com/smelter-labs/smelter-editor).
The prompts used in the episode are listed under "Example AI prompts" in the
repo's README.
