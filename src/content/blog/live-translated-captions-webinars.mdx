Transverb runs multilingual webinars where a single speaker reaches an audience
across a dozen languages. Client-side caption tracks worked, until they didn't:
recordings lost them, embeds stripped them, and mobile players rendered them
inconsistently.

## Captions as part of the frame

Instead of shipping a sidecar track, Transverb burns captions into the video
itself. A transcription service streams text into the pipeline, and a caption
overlay — an ordinary component — renders it over the speaker feed. Because the
subtitle is composited into the frame, it survives recording, embedding, and
every player that can show video.

Each language gets its own output. The speaker feed is composited once per
target language with a different caption overlay, and Smelter encodes each as a
separate WHEP stream. Attendees pick a language and get a video that already
has the right subtitles in it.

## Why burn them in

- Captions can't be stripped by an embed or lost in a recording.
- Styling is identical everywhere — same font, same safe-area, same timing.
- Adding a language is one more output, not a new player integration.

For a platform whose whole promise is "everyone understands," making the
captions part of the picture turned out to be the reliable path.
